# R1-M3 Signal Transformation Ablation — Source Spec

**Source:** User-provided prompt during `/gsd-discuss-phase 9` (2026-05-08).
Routed to `/gsd-insert-phase 9.1` for proper milestone integration.
**Reviewer item:** R1-M3 (signal transformation strips temporal structure; OD-scale evaluation missing; log-returns must be justified in bioprocess context).

## Reviewer concern

> "The combination of log-returns and Lambert W transformation appears to strip much of the temporal structure from the original signal. The near-flat ACF in Figure 6 is consistent with this concern — after these transformations the data may be reduced to weakly correlated increments, in which case generating them faithfully is comparatively easy and does not reflect the difficulty of bioprocess modeling. The manuscript reports metrics in the transformed domain but never inverts the transformation to evaluate fidelity on the original optical-density (OD) scale, which is the physically meaningful quantity. I recommend that results be reported on the original OD scale, with explicit demonstration of the inverse transformation, and that the use of log-returns be justified specifically in a bioprocess context — connected to a growth-rate interpretation — rather than imported uncritically from financial time-series."

## Goal

Run a controlled three-pipeline preprocessing ablation. Each pipeline trains QWGAN-GP with **identical** circuit architecture, hyperparameters, optimizer, epochs, and seeds. The only difference is the data transformation in and the inverse out. All evaluation metrics are computed on the OD scale so the three pipelines are directly comparable.

## The three pipelines

1. **Pipeline A — Normalized OD (control).** Min-max normalize raw OD trajectories to [0, 1]; train; un-normalize generated samples back to OD.
2. **Pipeline B — Log-returns only.** r_t = ln(OD_{t+1} / OD_t); standardize; train; un-standardize and integrate cumulatively from a known starting OD to recover OD trajectories.
3. **Pipeline C — Log-returns + Lambert W (current paper).** Existing pipeline as described in the manuscript.

## Evaluation (all on OD scale unless noted)

After generating samples and inverting back to OD space, compute for each pipeline:

- **Trajectory-level inspection:** sample real and synthetic trajectories overlaid; visual sanity check for biological plausibility (monotone growth phases, sigmoidal saturation).
- **Distributional fidelity:** PDF/CDF and Q-Q plot on OD values, pooled across time and per-time-step where useful.
- **Temporal structure:** ACF on OD trajectories — mean ACF across samples with ±1σ band. **This directly tests the reviewer's "strips temporal structure" claim.**
- **Dynamic similarity:** DTW distance distribution between each synthetic trajectory's nearest-neighbor real trajectory.
- **Utility (lightweight TSTR):** train a small LSTM forecaster on synthetic OD trajectories, evaluate on held-out real trajectories. Report MSE and R². Compare against a real-only training baseline.

Also report ACF and Q-Q in *transformed* space for B and C, as supplementary, to show the relationship between transformed-space and OD-space metrics.

## Run protocol

- **≥5 random seeds per pipeline.** Report mean ± std for every numerical metric.
- **Statevector simulator** (analytic expectation values); no shot noise.
- **Save all generated samples** (N_synth ≈ 10 × N_real per seed) as .npy or .parquet for later reuse.
- **Log all hyperparameters and seeds** to a YAML run config alongside results.
- **Smoke test first:** 1 seed, small epoch count, before launching the full run.

## Write-up at the end (`summary.md`)

Answer these four questions concisely, with numbers, for the rebuttal:

1. Does Pipeline A (raw normalized OD) train successfully and produce trajectories with preserved ACF and reasonable distributional fidelity? If yes, this answers the reviewer's implicit question of "why not just use OD."
2. Does Pipeline B (log-returns only) match or exceed A on OD-scale metrics? If yes, the log-return move is empirically justified — and the bioprocess interpretation (specific growth rate μ = d ln(OD)/dt) gives it a principled basis independent of finance.
3. Does Pipeline C (current paper) outperform B on OD-scale metrics? If yes, the Lambert W earns its keep. If no or marginally, we drop the Lambert W in the revision and report only the log-return pipeline.
4. Is OD-scale ACF preserved across all three pipelines? This is the direct empirical answer to "the transformation strips temporal structure" — the data either confirms or refutes it.

Include one paragraph proposing the recommended pipeline for the revised manuscript based on the results.
